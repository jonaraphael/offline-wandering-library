How it works

1

Plug in a drive.

Choose a USB stick, SD card or SSD in the library builder.

2

Press Sync.

OWL picks the best library for the space, downloads it and checks every file.

3

Put it in a drawer.

When you need it, open START_HERE.html from the drive.

Why it holds up

Works on what you have.

Opens in a web browser and a PDF viewer on Windows, Mac, Linux and Android. The guides are ordinary PDFs, so phones open them too.

The real pages.

Original documents from the WHO, Doctors Without Borders, Hesperian, FEMA and the USDA, diagrams included. Search results link to the source document or page.

The right things first.

Medicine, water, food and shelter fill first. The builder adjusts what it recommends to the space available.

Checked to the last byte.

Every file is verified by SHA-256 as it lands. Recheck the whole drive any time.

Yours to keep.

Free and open source. No account, no subscription. Make copies.

Questions

Why not just ask an AI?

An offline chatbot needs a recent, powerful computer, and it can be wrong. OWL opens on any computer with a browser and shows you the page a doctor or engineer wrote.

Will it open on my phone?

The guides are ordinary PDFs, so a phone’s file app opens them from the drive. Encyclopedias need the free Kiwix app; install it while you are online.

How do I keep it current?

Plug it in and press Sync. OWL checks for updated files. Use “Check every file” to verify the drive and download replacements for files that fail.

What does it cost?

Nothing. The builder is open source, and the documents are free from their publishers. You supply the drive.
